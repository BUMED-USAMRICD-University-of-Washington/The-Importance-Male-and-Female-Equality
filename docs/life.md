In human embryology, all individuals begin with identical, undifferentiated structures before genetic signals drive development toward male or female pathways. This developmental process results in biological homologues---structures that originate from the exact same embryonic precursor tissue. [1, 2, 3, 4]

The primary systems share identical internal organs like the stomach, intestines, and bladder. However, the structures that diverge to form specialized male and female anatomy map to specific embryonic origins. [3, 5, 6]

Key Systemic Insights
---------------------

-   The Nervous Systems and Fluid Circulation: The central nervous system, including the brain and the spinal cord, forms from a single continuous embryonic structure known as the neural tube. The cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) circulates continuously through the ventricles of the brain, down the central canal of the spinal cord, and throughout the subarachnoid space.
-   The Enteric Nervous System (The Gut): Often referred to in neuroscience as the "second brain" due to its massive network of independent neurons, the gut develops directly from the embryonic hindgut, midgut, and foregut structures. Primordial germ cells migrate along the wall of the hindgut near the yolk sac to reach the developing genital ridge where the gonads form. [7]
-   The Concept of Cis-Symmetry: In developmental biology, all organs are tracked by how these early cellular blueprints stretch, fold, and position themselves. For instance, the same tissue boundaries that fuse closed on the ventral surface of the penis in a male remain open to form the vestibule and labia minora in a female. [8, 9]
